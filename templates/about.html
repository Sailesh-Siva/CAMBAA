Bone Age Prediction

Bone age prediction estimates skeletal maturity from hand X-ray images and is primarily used for children and adolescents (0–19 years). Bone age is often compared with chronological age to evaluate whether growth and development are progressing normally.

This system was developed and evaluated using the RSNA Pediatric Bone Age Challenge dataset, a widely used benchmark dataset containing pediatric hand radiographs.

Applications

  • Monitoring growth rate and overall skeletal development.
  • Supporting endocrinologists in diagnosing growth and hormone (gland) related disorders.
  • Comparing a child's bone age with their chronological age to identify delayed or advanced skeletal development.
  • Assisting in sports age verification to help prevent age-related fraud.
  • Supporting forensic and criminal investigations where determining whether an individual is a minor is important.

BoneSight

BoneSight is an automated bone age assessment framework designed for pediatric hand X-ray analysis. The system combines YOLOv26-based hand localization, a MaxViT backbone enhanced with Residual CBAM attention, multimodal gender-aware feature fusion, and Support Vector Regression (SVR) for final bone age prediction. To improve interpretability, Eigen-CAM explainability is integrated to highlight the skeletal regions that contribute most significantly to each prediction.

BoneScale

BoneScale is a hybrid bone age assessment framework that combines anatomically derived skeletal measurements with deep learning-based image representations. Anatomical features are extracted through principal-axis geometric analysis of individual bones and carpal pixel-area measurements, while complementary deep features are generated using a MaxViT architecture enhanced with CBAM attention mechanisms. These feature sets are fused into a unified representation and processed using an XGBoost regression model to estimate pediatric bone age in months.
